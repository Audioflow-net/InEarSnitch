# Understanding the Live Position Guide

When seating an In-Ear Monitor (IEM) into the IEC 711 coupler, two completely different acoustic principles dictate the quality of your measurement: **Air-Tightness (Seal)** and **Insertion Depth**. 

The *InEar Snitch* Live Guide splits these into two independent visual indicators:

## 1. The Bass Line (40 Hz) — Checking the Seal
**Goal:** Ensure the IEM creates an air-tight seal in the coupler.
- **Why it matters:** If air escapes around the ear tip, acoustic pressure is lost, causing bass frequencies (below 100 Hz) to drop massively.
- **How to read it:**
  - 🟢 **Green Line (Bass Seal OK):** The system is air-tight. Bass frequencies are fully coupled.
  - 🔴 **Red Line (Bass Leak!):** Air is escaping. The bass has dropped significantly compared to the mid-range. 
- **How to fix a Leak:** Try a different ear tip size, check if the tip is deformed, or reseat the IEM straighter in the coupler.

## 2. The Treble Peak (~8 kHz) — Checking Insertion Depth
**Goal:** Ensure the distance between the IEM nozzle and the microphone matches the human ear canal standard.
- **Why it matters:** The coupler tube creates a resonance peak. The deeper you push the IEM, the shorter the tube, and the *higher* the resonance frequency shifts. The IEC standard dictates this peak should sit around 8 kHz.
- **How to read it:**
  - 🟡 **Push Deeper (Peak < 7 kHz):** The IEM is sitting too shallow. The tube is too long.
  - 🟢 **Depth OK (7 - 8.6 kHz):** The IEM is seated at the correct depth.
  - 🟡 **Pull Out Slightly (Peak > 8.6 kHz):** The IEM is shoved in too far.
- **Good to know:** Some deep-insertion IEMs (like Etymotic) will naturally resonate higher. In these cases, insert them until the flange sits properly, even if the peak goes above 8.6 kHz.
